Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to create and launch my first course? expand_more

Plan 3-6 months: Month 1 for curriculum design, months 2-4 for content creation and filming, month 5 for beta testing, and month 6 for launch preparation. Start with a minimum viable course (10-15 hours) and expand based on student feedback rather than waiting for perfection.

What credentials or qualifications do I need to teach fermentation? expand_more

Formal education (Siebel Institute, UC Davis) helps, but demonstrated expertise matters most. You need 3-5+ years hands-on experience, proven results, and clear teaching ability. Document your expertise through brewing logs, competition awards, or commercial experience. Students value practical skills over credentials alone.

How do I price my courses competitively? expand_more

Price based on outcomes, not content hours. Beginner courses: $49-$149 (exploring interest). Intermediate: $199-$449 (skill building). Professional: $599-$1,499+ (career advancement). Your structured curriculum, community, and support justify premium pricing over free content. Test with early cohorts and adjust based on conversion rates.

What's the best way to attract students initially? expand_more

Build audience first: publish free guides and YouTube tutorials, grow an email list with lead magnets, and contribute value in brewing communities. Launch with founding member discounts (30-40% off) for testimonials. Partner with equipment suppliers for referrals. Run live workshops to demonstrate expertise. Plan 6-12 months of audience building before launching paid courses.

How do I handle hands-on brewing instruction remotely? expand_more

Use structured assignments: students brew recipes and document with photos/video. Provide detailed multi-angle video demonstrations, live Q&A sessions, and community forums for peer learning. Optional in-person workshops add hands-on practice. Remote learning lets students progress at their own pace and rewatch complex techniques - often better than in-person classes.

Should I offer certification or credentials? expand_more

Yes, for professional-track students. Create clear outcomes with practical projects, written exams, and capstone demonstrations. Issue branded certificates showing completion. While not initially equivalent to Cicerone or Master Brewers certifications, it proves structured training. Emphasize practical portfolios: recipes, brewing logs, and demonstrated skills. Partner with industry associations for credential recognition as you grow.
